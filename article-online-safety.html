How to Stay Safe Online: Practical Tips Everyone Should Know

Learn how to protect your accounts, recognise online scams, safeguard your personal information and browse the internet more confidently.

The internet gives us opportunities to learn, communicate, work and access information. However, using online services also comes with risks. Scammers, fake websites, malicious downloads and stolen passwords can put your information and accounts at risk.

The good news is that you do not need to be a technology expert to improve your online safety. A few practical habits can make a meaningful difference.

1. Use strong and unique passwords

A password protects access to your online accounts. Using the same password everywhere creates a serious risk: if one service is compromised, attackers may try that password on your other accounts.

  • Use a different password for important accounts.
  • Choose long passwords or memorable passphrases.
  • Avoid obvious details such as your name or birthday.
  • Consider using a reputable password manager.

2. Enable two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication, also called 2FA, adds another security step when you sign in. Depending on the service, this may involve an authenticator app, security key or another verification method.

Enable it on important accounts such as your email, financial services and social media accounts whenever the option is available.

Important: Never share your verification codes, one-time passwords or account recovery codes with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

3. Learn to recognise phishing scams

Phishing is a form of online fraud in which criminals impersonate trusted organisations or individuals to trick people into revealing passwords, payment details or other sensitive information.

Be careful when a message:

  • Claims your account will be closed unless you act immediately.
  • Promises unexpected prizes or money.
  • Requests your password or verification code.
  • Contains suspicious links or unexpected attachments.
  • Pressures you to make an urgent payment.

If a message claims to come from a bank or online service, visit the organisation's official website or contact it through a trusted channel instead of following the message link.

4. Protect your personal information

Think carefully before sharing personal information online. Details such as your home address, phone number, financial information and identity documents should not be shared publicly without a good reason.

  • Review the privacy settings on your accounts.
  • Be selective about what you publish publicly.
  • Do not send identity documents to unverified contacts.
  • Check who can see your posts and personal details.

5. Keep your devices and applications updated

Software updates often include security improvements. Outdated applications and operating systems may contain weaknesses that attackers can exploit.

Install updates from official sources, enable automatic security updates where practical, and remove applications you no longer use.

6. Be careful with public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi in places such as airports, hotels and cafes can be convenient, but you should not automatically trust every available network.

  • Confirm the correct network name with the venue.
  • Avoid sensitive transactions on networks you do not trust.
  • Use websites that support HTTPS.
  • Turn off automatic connection to unfamiliar networks.

7. Download files from trusted sources

Unknown downloads can contain malicious software. Be cautious with free applications, cracked software, unexpected email attachments and files shared by strangers.

Download applications from official websites or trusted application stores. Keep security protection enabled on your device.

8. Back up important information

Backups can help you recover important documents and photographs if your device is lost, damaged or affected by malware.

Keep copies of important files in a secure cloud service or on a separate storage device. Protect backup accounts with strong passwords and additional authentication.

Build safer online habits

Online safety is not a one-time activity. It is a collection of habits that you practise whenever you use the internet. Pause before clicking unfamiliar links, verify unexpected requests and keep your accounts protected.

Your safety checklist:
  • Use unique, strong passwords.
  • Enable two-factor authentication.
  • Verify suspicious messages and links.
  • Protect personal information.
  • Update your devices and applications.
  • Back up important files.
